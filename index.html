Method, in brief

Both uses of biomass are put on a common metric: dollars per tonne of CO₂ climate benefit. For SAF the benefit is fossil jet fuel displaced; for CDR it is CO₂ permanently removed; FT-SAF + CCS earns both.

Cost formulas

SAF: (MFSP × gallons − jet fuel revenue − 45Z credit − 45Q on stored CO₂) ÷ tCO₂ benefit. MFSP (minimum fuel selling price) is feedstock-inclusive. Gallons per dry tonne (1,000 kg) follow from mass yield (kg fuel / kg feedstock ÷ 0.8 kg/L ÷ 3.79 L/gal). Under full lifecycle accounting (default) each gallon avoids its lifecycle GHG reduction × 11.7 kg CO₂e, the CORSIA well-to-wake fossil jet baseline (89 gCO₂e/MJ); under simple displacement it avoids the full 11.7 kg, as if the SAF itself had zero lifecycle emissions. FT-SAF + CCS is not priced independently: its MFSP is the FT-SAF (ag/forest) cost plus the CCS cost slider applied to the ~0.018 tCO₂ stored per gallon (54% of biomass carbon exits as a concentrated CO₂ stream during gasification, so capture is cheap; the cost is mostly compression, transport and storage, default $70/t, P10–P90 $40–120).

CDR: (total cost − co-product revenue − 45Q) ÷ tCO₂ removed. Costs are feedstock-inclusive and calibrated to actual offtake pricing. A dry tonne of biomass (50% carbon) holds 1.83 tCO₂e; removal = 1.83 × capture efficiency.

Subsidies

The 45Q credit ($85/tCO₂ statutory) requires gaseous CO₂ capture: BECCS, WtE+CCS, and FT-SAF+CCS qualify; bio-oil, biochar, burial, and injection do not. The 45Z Clean Fuel Production Credit (post-OBBBA: up to $1.00/gal with prevailing-wage compliance, through 2029; feedstock must come from the US, Canada or Mexico) is scaled by carbon intensity, (50 − CI)/50, with CI floored at zero. At the default lifecycle reductions it is worth ~$0.81/gal for FT-SAF, ~$0.59/gal for HEFA and ~$0.34/gal for AtJ, and it varies with each Monte Carlo draw. A facility claiming 45Q is not a qualified 45Z facility (§45Z(d)(4)), so FT-SAF + CCS carries an election: 45Q on stored CO₂ (default, worth more) or 45Z alone with CCS counted in the fuel's CI.

The 45V clean hydrogen production credit survived OBBBA but was shortened: facilities must begin construction before January 1, 2028 (previously 2033) to claim up to $3.00/kg for ten years. 45V and 45Q cannot be claimed at the same facility (§45V(d)(2)), so the BECCS (hydrogen) pathway carries an election toggle: 45Q on stored CO₂ (default) or 45V on the hydrogen, worth ~$126/tCO₂ removed at the default 65% capture efficiency. Deeply carbon-negative biomass-gasification hydrogen readily meets the 0.45 kgCO₂e/kg threshold for the full rate. Its co-product revenue default ($130/tCO₂ ≈ $3.1/kg H₂) is a market price between gray and green hydrogen, independent of either credit.

Accounting toggles

Full lifecycle (default) multiplies SAF's benefit by its lifecycle GHG reduction (CORSIA defaults, 55–91% depending on pathway), applies 95% net-negativity to CDR storage, and credits CDR co-products (electricity, heat, hydrogen) with the fossil emissions they displace. Simple displacement is an upper bound for SAF: each gallon is credited with the full well-to-wake emissions of the fossil jet it replaces, as if producing the SAF emitted nothing, and CDR storage is counted gross. In both modes the lifecycle reduction still sets the 45Z credit, which the law ties to the fuel's actual carbon intensity.

Social cost of carbon lines

The dashed reference lines are EPA's 2023 estimates of the social cost of CO₂ for 2025 emissions (Report on the Social Cost of Greenhouse Gases, Table A.5.1): $130 at a 2.5% discount rate and $360 at 1.5% (central $212 at 2.0%), in 2020 dollars, shown here as $159 and $441 in 2025 dollars using the GDP price deflator. Independent estimates fall in a similar range: Rennert et al. (2022) find a mean of $185 (5–95%: $44–413, 2020$), and a meta-analysis by Moore et al. (2024) a median of $185. These are not the values US agencies currently apply: Executive Order 14154 (January 2025) withdrew the interagency estimates, and OMB memo M-25-27 (May 2025) states that monetized greenhouse gas analysis will be needed in "few to none" cases.

Caveats

Avoided ≠ removed: SAF benefits are counterfactual and shrink if aviation decarbonizes another way; CDR removals are permanent. Non-CO₂ effects are off by default; the SAF non-CO₂ credit slider adds them as a sensitivity. For 100% SAF, contrail forcing falls ~26–44% (Märkl et al. 2024; Teoh et al. 2022); with Lee et al. (2021) contrail CO₂-equivalence ratios that is worth roughly +16–28% of SAF's CO₂ benefit under GWP100 and +46–78% under GWP*. No commercial FT-SAF+CCS plant exists; those costs are techno-economic estimates. HEFA uses waste fats and oils, which mostly do not compete with cellulosic CDR for the same feedstock; global collection of used cooking oil and animal fats is roughly 25 Mt/yr (IEA 2023) and already largely committed to biofuels.
